How the AI thinks

Each of the 10 players is its own agent. It looks at the court several times a second and picks what to do.

With the ball, a player scores every option: shoot, pass to each teammate, drive to the rim, or hold. Each score is roughly the expected points: the chance of making that shot times 2 or 3. Passes are marked down when a defender is near the passing lane. The team's game plan nudges the scores, and a late shot clock makes shooting more urgent.

Without the ball, offense players space out, and smart ones cut to the basket when their defender looks away. Defenders stay between their player and the basket, or guard an area in a zone. Everyone chases missed shots.

Basketball IQ controls how often a player picks the best option. Low IQ adds random noise to the scores, so that player makes more mistakes.
